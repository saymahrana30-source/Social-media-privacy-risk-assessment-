Learn: privacy, security and social engineering

Privacy vs security

Privacy controls how personal information is collected, shared, exposed and used. Security protects systems, accounts and information from unauthorised access or misuse.

A strong password with MFA protects the account, but if your phone number, birthday and travel plans are public, your privacy is still weak. Strong account security is not the same as strong privacy.

Digital footprint

Active: things you deliberately share. Passive: data generated or collected through your activity. This project only evaluates self-reported practices - old posts, old accounts, public comments, profile history.

Oversharing and social engineering

Social engineering manipulates people rather than systems. Public details such as employer, college, travel plans, family references and interests can make a scam message sound believable. Defence: share less, verify requests through a second channel, never share verification codes.

Location exposure

Real-time posts show where you are now (and that home may be empty). Posting a photo after you have left the place reduces immediate exposure compared with broadcasting live location.

Photos and metadata (EXIF)

Photos can carry hidden details: device, time, sometimes GPS. Backgrounds can reveal badges, plates, documents or screens. A local tool is included: tools/metadata_viewer.py (nothing is uploaded).

MFA and least privilege

MFA adds a second proof of identity, so a stolen password alone is not enough. Least privilege means apps and people get only the access they need - review and revoke the rest.

Public visibility is not automatically unsafe; it increases the amount of information potentially accessible. Settings should be reviewed regularly because platforms change defaults.
