# How oversharing can increase social-engineering risk (defensive awareness)

Social engineering succeeds when a message feels *believable*. Believability often comes from information people publish voluntarily:

| Public information | Why it matters (concept) |
|---|---|
| Employer | Makes a "message from HR/IT/your manager" seem plausible |
| College | Makes "alumni", "admissions" or "scholarship" messages seem plausible |
| Travel plans | Signals you are away; a "friend stuck abroad" story seems plausible |
| Family references | Supplies names for "relative in trouble" stories |
| Interests | Supplies hooks for fake giveaways and communities |
| Public events | Supplies timely pretexts (tickets, registrations) |

**Defences:** share the minimum; keep contact details and birth year private; accept connections only after verification; confirm urgent requests through a second channel (call a known number); never share verification codes; check links before clicking; report and block suspicious accounts; review audience settings regularly.

This project deliberately contains no attack messages or scripts and does not personalise anything against a real person.
